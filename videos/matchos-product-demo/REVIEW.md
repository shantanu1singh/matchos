# Review — multilingual product walkthrough

## Creative decision

The opening is one continuous real 1x recording of the authenticated deployed app. The broadcaster and analyst choose press breakdown, while the Liverpool fan chooses momentum in Spanish. The genuine fan Listen response plays while the English narrator is silent. Architecture, telemetry/grading, deployment and a clearly marked roadmap follow. Transparent outlined SVG wordmarks replace screenshot logos in both video projects.

## Verified

The composition and English narration are exactly 120 seconds. The source take is 53 seconds after trimming setup; its first 52 seconds are used without internal cuts or speed changes. Judge demo launches at 4.83 seconds; all three personas finish at 13.86 seconds. Actual Spanish app audio plays at 29.422–33.950 seconds, before English narration resumes at 34.450. Microsoft en-GB-Emily MAI-Voice-2.1 uses narrator delivery; source speech remains in its actual Spanish Microsoft voice. Sound was checked through timing, duration, waveform/loudness and transcription, not auditioned by ear.

Full HyperFrames check: zero lint, runtime, layout, motion or contrast errors. Five craft warnings remain for small secondary labels and reading holds. Beat sheets, phone sheet, all transition strips and native snapshots at language settings, persona comparison, Listen, explanation, facts, pitch evidence, architecture, Azure and closing were inspected. No unreadable logo, background rectangle or text clipping remains in the current cut. No grading scores are fabricated. Telemetry is an explicitly labeled separate captured run.

## Deliberate limits

The native desktop fact table is the weakest reading shot, especially at phone width. This landscape demo targets desktop judging. A separate vertical edit would need a dedicated crop and fewer simultaneous UI elements. The real model wait remains intact. Secondary monospace labels below 28px and the intentional hold are accepted; principal headings remain large. Native browser option menus are not part of browser video capture, but the real Spanish selection is visible and the four available languages are named in narration and deployment graphics.
